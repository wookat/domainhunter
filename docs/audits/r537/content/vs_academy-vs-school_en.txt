.academy vs .school
.academy vs .school: Structured Curriculum or Everyday Classroom
Which to pick
Both are Identity Digital education suffixes, but they teach in different registers. .academy says structure — the word carries a sense of organized, professional curriculum, an association Khan Academy pushed to its peak: online course platforms, coding bootcamps, corporate training and brand academies on name.academy promise a program, not a single lesson; about $12 to register and $38/yr to renew. .school says everyday — the most literal education suffix there is, name.school reads as exactly what it is, warm and approachable: kindergartens, after-school programs, driving and dance schools, and hobby classes aimed at kids and parents fit perfectly, and learn-a-craft projects (the sourdough.school pattern) charm on it too; about $6 to register and $29/yr to renew — cheaper on both ends. The test: a structured curriculum for adults or companies, positioning as professional advancement → .academy's register fits; a kids-and-parents audience, a warm tone, or a tight budget → .school does more with less. Shared cautions: neither is an accreditation — formal credentials belong on the page; and both are long suffixes (seven and six letters), so keep the subject word short. Both suffixes are sponsored by Binky Moon, LLC and operated on Identity Digital's platform — .academy delegated in December 2013 among the first keyword gTLDs, .school in February 2015 — so policy, premium tiers and dispute handling are identical, and neither requires accreditation of any kind. That last point is the trap on the .school side: in many countries the word school carries legal weight (registered schools, inspection regimes, child-safeguarding rules), so an unaccredited course brand on .school may draw questions that the same brand on .academy would not. Academy, by contrast, has been a loose, unregulated word for centuries — riding academies, police academies, Khan Academy — and no regulator expects an academy to be a school. The words also differ in what they imply about the learner: school suggests a stage of life, mostly children and teenagers, while academy suggests a chosen discipline pursued at any age. Use-case split: a coding bootcamp, a sales-training programme inside a company, a music or dance academy for adults, a certification prep course, a martial-arts dojo → .academy; an actual primary or secondary school, a language school with a physical campus, a driving school, an after-school tutoring centre, a homeschooling co-op → .school. A company that runs both an employee academy and a public course catalogue can keep them on the two suffixes and avoid confusing HR with customers.
