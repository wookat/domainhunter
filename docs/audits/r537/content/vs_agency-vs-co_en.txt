.agency vs .co: Self-Describing Suffix or Sleek Company Shorthand
Which to pick
The classic dilemma for service firms: explanatory power or brevity. .agency introduces you by itself — design studios, marketing teams, PR and recruiting firms on name.agency need no tagline, freeing the root to be bold and abstract; the cost is a six-character suffix that must be spoken carefully. .co is two characters, reads as shorthand for company, and feels closest to .com; the cost is permanent leak risk — users who type the missing "m" land on whoever owns the .com, so check that owner before committing. On price, .co registers around $10 but renews notably higher than .com ($25–35 at many registrars); .agency runs cheap promos then renews around $20–25 — long-term costs end up similar. Inventory strongly favors .agency: nearly any word style is still available. The test: if clients come via search and referrals and you want the suffix to explain the business → .agency; if your brand word is distinctive and you want business-card sleekness → .co. The registries could hardly differ more. .agency is a generic TLD delegated in January 2014 to Binky Moon, LLC and run on Identity Digital's platform under an ICANN registry agreement with no eligibility rules. .co is Colombia's country code, delegated in December 1991, overseen by the Colombian ICT ministry (MinTIC) with day-to-day operations run by a commercial operator it appoints; it is marketed globally and open to anyone, but its rules are ccTLD policy, not an ICANN contract, and restricted names such as .gov.co or .edu.co must go through a local Colombian registrar. For an agency that means .agency's future tracks a large US registry portfolio, while .co's tracks a national concession — different kinds of predictability, neither obviously worse. The more practical divergence is typing: .co is one keystroke away from .com, so a share of your traffic will leak to whoever owns name.com unless you also hold it; .agency has no such neighbour, and its length makes accidental typos rarer. Use-case split: a creative shop that bills by project, a PR or influencer agency, a recruitment firm, a real-estate agency, a talent agency — anything where clients need to know the kind of service before they click → .agency; a firm that will grow into a product company, a consultancy with a coined one-word name, a brand that wants to read as a company rather than a vendor, a startup for whom the .com is gone → .co, ideally alongside the matching .com.
