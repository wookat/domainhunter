.academy vs .coach
.academy vs .coach: A Curriculum or a Person
Which to pick
Both are in the business of making people better; they sell different products. .academy sells the program — name.academy promises an organized curriculum: online course platforms, coding bootcamps, corporate training and brand academies use it when the product is a teaching system with scale, multiple instructors and a syllabus; about $12 to register and $38/yr to renew, good value among training suffixes. .coach sells the person — name.coach promises the one-on-one trainer themselves: personal trainers, life coaches and executive coaches acquire clients on personal trust, and writing the identity into the domain (your name or niche + .coach) beats any slogan; registration (~$11) matches .academy, but renewal around $62/yr is clearly higher — budget on the renewal. The test: the product is a course (replicable, scalable, content-led) → .academy's institutional register fits and costs less to keep; the product is you (one-on-one, small groups, accountability and feedback) → .coach's identity signal is sharper. The growth path is common too: a solo coach who scales into a course brand graduates from .coach to .academy — the two suffixes map to two stages of the same business. Shared cautions: neither certifies anything — display coaching credentials (ICF etc.) and course outcomes on the page; and both are long-ish (seven and five letters), so keep the front word short. Both suffixes are sponsored by Binky Moon, LLC and run on Identity Digital's platform — .academy delegated in December 2013, .coach in November 2014 — so the registry policy, premium tiers and dispute handling are identical, and neither asks for accreditation. That leaves the words, which encode two different business models. Academy is an institution: it implies a curriculum, cohorts, a place (even a virtual one) and, usually, more than one instructor, so it scales to a team and to recorded courses without the domain feeling wrong. Coach is a person: it implies a relationship, live sessions and a calendar with limited slots, so it caps naturally at what one practitioner (or a small roster) can deliver. That difference has a pricing consequence — academy names support product pricing (a course costs X), coach names support time pricing (a session costs Y) — and a hiring consequence, because a .coach brand built on one founder is hard to hand to a second coach, while a .academy brand absorbs new instructors without a rename. Use-case split: an online course library, a bootcamp with cohorts, a corporate training programme, a sports academy with age-group squads, a certification prep school → .academy; a solo executive coach, a running or strength coach with a monthly programme, a language tutor, a public-speaking coach, a career coach for one profession → .coach. Coaches who want to grow into an institution usually launch on .coach and register the .academy the day they hire their second instructor.
