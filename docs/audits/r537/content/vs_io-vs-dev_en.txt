.io vs .dev
.io vs .dev: The Veteran Hacker Suffix or the Purpose-Built One
Which to pick
Both are mainstream choices for a developer brand; the difference lies in origin and rules. .io is the country code of the British Indian Ocean Territory, delegated in 1997 and managed by Internet Computer Bureau, now listed care of Sure (Diego Garcia); the registry site nic.io says residency in the territory is not required, and its standing was built over more than a decade by products such as itch.io, socket.io and fly.io. .dev is a generic TLD run by Google's Charleston Road Registry, delegated in November 2014, with the whole zone on the browser HSTS preload list — a .dev site without a certificate will not load — and Google's own web.dev plus the registry showcase's arc.dev and mdn.dev are public examples. Those two facts produce three practical differences. First, the security default: enforced HTTPS on .dev is a registry rule that every .dev name inherits, while .io has no such mechanism and leaves it to the site owner. Second, governance and risk: .dev operates under an ICANN generic-TLD agreement, whereas .io's rules are set by the territory's manager and the UK–Mauritius Chagos treaty signed in May 2025 adds a conditional risk — if it enters into force and changes the ISO country code, ICANN's ccTLD retirement policy applies with a five-year transition, though no timetable exists today. Third, price: in the site's reference table .io is high-tier on both first year and renewal, while .dev sits in the stable hundred-yuan tier, a multiple apart (figures below). Decide this way: if the product courts the venture scene, wants the veteran hacker aesthetic of .io, and the team accepts the renewals and the geopolitical variable, take .io; if the product is a developer tool, a docs site or a personal engineering brand and you value enforced HTTPS and predictable long-term cost, take .dev. Short words are far more available than on .com either way, and .dev, being younger, still has two- and three-syllable real words open. Teams already on .io need not migrate, but should put renewal and policy risk on the annual review; for new projects .dev is the steadier default today, and a .io pointing back at the main site can be added later if the insider cachet is ever needed.
